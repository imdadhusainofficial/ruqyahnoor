Qur'anic Verses Commonly Used in Ruqyah

Explore Quranic passages commonly recited in Ruqyah Shariah, their references, and practical guidance based on the Qur'an and authentic Sunnah.

Home / Articles / Qur'anic Verses for Ruqyah

The Qur'an is a source of guidance, mercy, and healing by Allah's permission. This guide introduces several Quranic passages commonly recited in Ruqyah Shariah, along with their references and practical guidance based on the Qur'an and authentic Sunnah.

What Is Ruqyah with the Qur'an?

Ruqyah Shariah is the recitation of Quranic verses and authentic supplications while asking Allah for protection, relief, and healing. Muslims place their trust in Allah, who alone grants healing.

Surah Al-Isra (17:82)

وَنُنَزِّلُ مِنَ الْقُرْآنِ مَا هُوَ شِفَاءٌ وَرَحْمَةٌ لِلْمُؤْمِنِينَ

Meaning: The Qur'an describes itself as a healing and mercy for the believers.

Read the full verse: Qur'an 17:82.

1. Surah Al-Fatihah

Surah Al-Fatihah is one of the most important passages recited in Ruqyah. In an authentic narration, a companion recited Al-Fatihah over a person who had been bitten or stung, and the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ approved of it as Ruqyah.

Qur'an: Surah Al-Fatihah (1:1–7)

2. Ayat al-Kursi

Ayat al-Kursi is verse 255 of Surah Al-Baqarah. It affirms Allah's absolute authority, knowledge, and greatness. It is widely recited for remembrance and protection.

Qur'an: Surah Al-Baqarah (2:255)

Read Ayat al-Kursi

For its virtue when recited before sleeping, see Sahih al-Bukhari 2311 .

3. The Last Two Verses of Surah Al-Baqarah

The final two verses of Surah Al-Baqarah are verses 285 and 286. They contain expressions of faith, supplication, and reliance upon Allah.

Qur'an: Surah Al-Baqarah (2:285–286)

Read the last two verses of Al-Baqarah

The Prophet ﷺ taught that whoever recites the last two verses of Surah Al-Baqarah at night, they will be sufficient for him.

Hadith reference: Sahih Muslim 807a.

4. Surah Al-Ikhlas, Surah Al-Falaq, and Surah An-Nas

These three short chapters are commonly known as the three Quls. They are especially important in the authentic Sunnah for seeking Allah's protection.

Surah Al-Ikhlas (112)

This chapter teaches the oneness and uniqueness of Allah. Read Surah Al-Ikhlas .

Surah Al-Falaq (113)

This chapter teaches us to seek refuge in Allah from various forms of harm, including the evil of envy. Read Surah Al-Falaq .

Surah An-Nas (114)

This chapter teaches us to seek Allah's protection from evil whispers that affect people. Read Surah An-Nas .

Authentic Sunnah: Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) reported that the Prophet ﷺ would recite Surah Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq, and An-Nas, blow lightly into his hands, and wipe them over his body before sleeping. The narration describes repeating this practice three times. See Sahih al-Bukhari 5017 .

5. Other Quranic Verses About Healing

The following verses are also relevant when reflecting on the Qur'an's message of healing, mercy, and Allah's power to cure.

  • Surah Yunus (10:57): The Qur'an describes guidance, mercy, and healing for what is within people's hearts. Read verse 10:57.
  • Surah Ash-Shu'ara (26:80): Prophet Ibrahim (peace be upon him) says that when he becomes ill, Allah is the One who cures him. Read verse 26:80.
  • Surah Fussilat (41:44): This verse describes the Qur'an as guidance and healing for those who believe. Read verse 41:44.

These verses provide valuable reminders about healing and reliance upon Allah. Their mention here should not be understood as a claim that each verse has a separately established, specific Ruqyah procedure or guaranteed outcome.

How to Recite These Verses for Ruqyah

  1. Begin with sincere faith in Allah and ask Him alone for help and healing.
  2. Recite Quranic passages clearly and respectfully, using a reliable Arabic text if you need help with pronunciation.
  3. You may recite Al-Fatihah, Ayat al-Kursi, the last two verses of Al-Baqarah, and the three Quls as part of your Quranic recitation and supplication.
  4. Understand the meaning of what you recite and make sincere du'a to Allah.
  5. Avoid superstitious rituals, unknown incantations, and claims of guaranteed cures or certain results.

For a practical step-by-step guide, read How to Perform Self-Ruqyah.

Important Islamic Guidance

Ruqyah should remain within the limits of Islamic teachings. The Prophet ﷺ said that there is no harm in Ruqyah so long as it does not involve shirk (associating partners with Allah). See Sahih Muslim 2200 .

Ruqyah is not a substitute for appropriate medical care. If you experience ongoing physical or mental health symptoms, seek advice from a qualified healthcare professional while continuing lawful worship and supplication.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Surah is commonly recited for Ruqyah?

Surah Al-Fatihah is explicitly established as Ruqyah in an authentic hadith. Other Quranic passages, including the three Quls, are also supported by authentic Islamic narrations.

Can I perform Ruqyah on myself?

Yes. Muslims can recite Quranic verses and authentic supplications for themselves and ask Allah for protection and healing. Read our self-Ruqyah guide for more information.

Does reciting these verses guarantee a cure?

No particular worldly outcome should be guaranteed. Muslims seek healing from Allah, while recognizing that healing occurs according to His wisdom. Appropriate medical treatment should not be neglected.

Conclusion

Surah Al-Fatihah, Ayat al-Kursi, the last two verses of Surah Al-Baqarah, and the three Quls are important Quranic passages for worship, remembrance, and seeking Allah's protection. Recite them with sincerity, learn their meanings, and follow authentic Islamic guidance without unsupported claims or practices.
