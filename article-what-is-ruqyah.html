What Is Ruqyah Shariah? A Guide from the Quran and Sunnah

Ruqyah Shariah is the practice of seeking Allah’s protection and help through Quranic recitation, authentic supplications, and words that are permissible in Islam. This guide explains its meaning, essential principles, and practical approach based on the Quran and authentic Sunnah.

1. What Does Ruqyah Mean?

Ruqyah refers to recitations and supplications used to seek Allah’s protection and ask Him for relief from harm, illness, distress, and other difficulties. Ruqyah Shariah means Ruqyah practiced in accordance with Islamic teachings.

Muslims believe that Allah alone controls benefit and harm. Quranic verses and supplications are means of seeking His help; they do not work independently of His will.

2. Evidence for Ruqyah in the Quran

Allah describes the Quran as a source of healing and mercy for believers.

Quran — Surah Al-Isra, 17:82

The verse explains that Allah sends down from the Quran that which is healing and mercy for the believers.

Reference: Read the verse on Quran.com

The Quran also records Prophet Ibrahim’s statement that Allah is the One who provides healing when he becomes ill.

Quran — Surah Ash-Shu'ara, 26:80

“And when I am ill, it is He who cures me.”

Reference: Read the verse on Quran.com

3. Evidence for Ruqyah in the Sunnah

Authentic hadith reports show that the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ permitted Ruqyah as long as it did not involve shirk (associating partners with Allah).

Sahih Muslim, Hadith 2200

The report explains that Ruqyah is permissible when it does not contain shirk.

Reference: Read the hadith on Sunnah.com

This establishes an important principle: Ruqyah must remain within the boundaries of Islamic belief and practice.

4. Essential Conditions of Ruqyah Shariah

A Muslim should observe the following principles when performing or seeking Ruqyah:

  • It should involve the Quran, authentic supplications, or permissible words with a clear meaning.
  • It must not contain shirk, invocations to jinn, or requests for help in ways that contradict Islamic teachings.
  • The person performing Ruqyah and the person receiving it should believe that healing and protection come from Allah alone.
  • Unknown formulas, claims of guaranteed results, fortune-telling, and claims of certain knowledge of the unseen should be avoided.

These principles help distinguish permissible Ruqyah from practices that conflict with Islamic teachings.

5. How Can You Perform Self-Ruqyah?

Muslims can perform Ruqyah for themselves by reciting Quranic passages and authentic supplications while sincerely asking Allah for protection and healing.

  1. Begin by praising Allah and asking Him sincerely for help.
  2. Recite Surah Al-Fatihah.
  3. Recite Ayat al-Kursi (Quran 2:255).
  4. Recite Surah Al-Ikhlas, Surah Al-Falaq, and Surah An-Nas.
  5. Make authentic supplications from the Sunnah and ask Allah for well-being and relief.
  6. Maintain regular prayer, remembrance of Allah, and other acts of worship.

These are general examples of Quranic recitation and supplication. Do not treat Ruqyah as a mechanical formula or assume that a particular result is guaranteed.

6. When Should Someone Seek Ruqyah?

A Muslim may make Quranic recitation and supplication part of their regular worship and may seek Ruqyah when looking for spiritual support. It should not be assumed that every illness, family problem, anxiety, or difficulty is caused by the evil eye, magic, or jinn.

Avoid making accusations or drawing conclusions about unseen causes without reliable evidence. Focus on worship, sound Islamic guidance, and practical steps that address the actual problem.

7. Ruqyah and Medical Care

Ruqyah and medical treatment are not mutually exclusive. Seeking appropriate medical advice, diagnosis, and treatment is important when someone experiences physical or mental health symptoms.

Do not stop prescribed medication or delay urgent medical care because of Ruqyah. Spiritual practices should complement, not replace, appropriate healthcare.

Conclusion

Ruqyah Shariah is a permissible means of seeking Allah’s protection and help through Quranic recitation and appropriate supplications. It must remain free from shirk and prohibited practices, and it should be accompanied by sound judgment and appropriate medical care when needed.

Muslims should learn Ruqyah from reliable Quranic and Sunnah sources, avoid exaggerated claims, and place their trust in Allah.
